## Notes a reader needs beside the verdict

**The ratchet treats zipformer differently.** Ours re-decode every window from scratch, so a word gets several looks; the `checks-w1` arm gives zipformer the same shape (one fresh inference per check, 1 s of warm state, warm-up emissions discarded, the 1.05 s flush kept). It is the pre-registered verdict arm; the single-stream `checks` and the windows-only variant are report-only.

**W = 1 s** is fixed before any verdict set was decoded: it is the hop the app has just committed, so a deployment keeps one extra hop of buffer; zipformer's left context is about 5.1 s, so a much longer warm-up would converge on the single stream and lose the looks; and #148 measured 1 s of left context as outcome-neutral for a model of our kind.

**The single-stream `checks` arm is causal, not sliced from the future.** An emission's text depends on audio up to the end of its chunk; offline slicing changes when it would arrive (by at most one 0.48 s call), not what it says.

**Neither `likely_hallucination` nor `strip_padding_artifacts` is applied.** Both guard our model's zero-padded, zero-state windows; zipformer never decodes a padded window, and applying them would delete genuine edge letters.

**v3.2's card-gate numbers are a v1-protocol baseline**, reported beside the gate table only; the card's table describes v3, which never appears in a comparison set.

**qdat_bench is not read.** Its 159 clips are qdat's own bytes, so their decodes are qdat's; only its expert `phonetic_transcript` would add, and that read needs the gated Hub token, which lives on cuda-dev. The qdat side scores every model against the phonetizer reference over the mushaf, as the other readouts do.

**What the screen cannot separate.** qdat and the benchmark's tlog_holdout carry zipformer's known text prior (its card admits 39.5% of planted haraka mistakes reported, and ikhfnaa written on 81 of 94 plain-noon clips); the catch side is where that shows, and it is why the verdict is read on it.
