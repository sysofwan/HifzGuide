## Look diversity (explanation, never a gate)

## Notes a reader needs beside the verdict

**The ratchet treats zipformer differently.** Ours re-decode every window from scratch, so a word gets several looks; the `checks-w1` arm gives zipformer the same shape (one fresh inference per check, 1 s of warm state, warm-up emissions discarded, the 1.05 s flush kept). It is the pre-registered verdict arm; the single-stream `checks` and the windows-only variant are report-only.

**W = 1 s** is fixed before any verdict set was decoded: it is the hop the app has just committed, so a deployment keeps one extra hop of buffer; zipformer's left context is about 5.1 s, so a much longer warm-up would converge on the single stream and lose the looks; and #148 measured 1 s of left context as outcome-neutral for a model of our kind.

**The single-stream `checks` arm is causal, not sliced from the future.** An emission's text depends on audio up to the end of its chunk; offline slicing changes when it would arrive (by at most one 0.48 s call), not what it says.

**Neither `likely_hallucination` nor `strip_padding_artifacts` is applied.** Both guard our model's zero-padded, zero-state windows; zipformer never decodes a padded window, and applying them would delete genuine edge letters.

**Both card-gate runs are v1-protocol, and both predate the repair.** The gate's round-trip is the card's own v1 tokenizer rule, and both runs decoded with the zero-padded final chunk the diagnosis round removed. v3.2's numbers are a baseline (the card's table is v3's, which never appears in a comparison set), and v3's STOP (570/577 = 98.79%, 4 of 7 failures a rule-segmentation the longest-match stand-in invents) is kept as the record of what that decoder did, not re-run here. The reference-runtime parity gate replaced them, and it is the one that decides.

**The int8 export is a numerics line only.** Its single piece of evidence is the identity's pinned SHA-256; no int8 decode is committed, compared or used, and no call in this screen loads it.

**qdat_bench reads the experts' transcript, not their audio twice.** Its clips are qdat's own bytes, so the decodes are the models' qdat decodes; what the corpus adds is the expert `phonetic_transcript`, which is read where the gated Hub token is and never committed. Only the agreement summary is in this record.

**The report-only sets read one arm per model and the whole population.** QUD's unread half and both of Tarteel v1's pre-registered halves are scored at the carrier from each arm's committed transcript: ours from the app's own per-check decodes, zipformer from its single stream, because that is the only store those corpora carry. All four models decode every item of each set; an arm missing one refuses the table rather than shrinking the population. qdat_bench reads the arm the arm map prescribes (ours `stream_b0`, zipformer `spans`), so its expert agreement is on the same text as every other whole-item metric.

**What the screen cannot separate.** qdat and the benchmark's tlog_holdout carry zipformer's known text prior (its card admits 39.5% of planted haraka mistakes reported, and ikhfnaa written on 81 of 94 plain-noon clips); the catch side is where that shows, and it is why the verdict is read on it.
